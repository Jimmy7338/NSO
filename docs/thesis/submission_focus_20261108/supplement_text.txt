6.2

Surface reference and fairness

The evaluator constructs the exterior boundary of the simulator axis-aligned box union, removing internal and duplicate coplanar faces. It draws 100,000 area-weighted samples with seed 20261119; a
fixed 0.8 m lattice of start-reachable camera origins and a first-hit ray test retain the sensor-observable
reference. The reference permits arbitrary planar yaw and is an approximation to observation opportunity on that fixed lattice, not all exterior surface. D retains 56,620 reference points. Precision uses
12,000 area-weighted reconstructed samples with seed 812 and distance to exterior triangles; recall
uses the reverse distance from the fixed reference to the reconstructed mesh. Thresholds are 5 and 10
cm. This is a local adaptation of thresholded surface comparison, not an official benchmark score. No
geometric alignment to the reference is performed.
All arms share 512-action caps, return and collision rules, candidate generation, initial packet
and per-layout reference hashes. C is a coverage-only score on the same candidate generator, not an
external frontier implementation. G2 multiplies the generic prior by two without scaling the public
common term. The frozen-gain arm retains camera history, direction observations and mapping. Every
completed attempt and its artifacts are hashed; the development G mesh is additionally reproduced
from all 513 packets, with identical coverage at every frame and identical final mesh vertices and
triangles. Known-cell coverage can include false occupied labels on the free reference, so the records
also retain those errors and per-room coverage. Coverage-curve area uses trapezoidal integration over
the common budget, holding the final observation after an early stop without inventing actions.

analysis of S states, not an additional weighted trajectory.

Predicted and realized surface benefit

In D, G/S have identical complete traces. Replaying the 98-action segment from action 366 to 464
yields zero reference coverage gain but raises 5 cm recall from 79.1169% to 80.2490% and F1 from
88.1847% to 88.9299%. It adds 886 measured 15 cm patch keys; among the fixed 1,600 old points
used by the quality proxy, 241 acquire a new direction and 255 improve measured information. Patch
counts are not surface area or accuracy. Holding the starting assets and route fixed while updating only
their measured direction support reduces generic prior value from 7.2256 to 5.7445 and category prior
value from 9.4781 to 7.8134. The prior is consumed partially, leaving a large residual. The preceding
92-action segment gains 4.2476 coverage percentage points and 4.1613 F1 points, so local observation
benefit does not establish efficient allocation.
The coverage posteriors at action 366 are 0.97778 for radar and 0.07990 for camera. These are
conditional yields of predicted cells, not calibrated sensor correctness: the episode realizes 483 of
493 predicted radar events and 30 of 386 predicted camera events, while also recording 3,438 and
952 unpredicted new-cell events. The old-quality score deduplicates route observations by pointwise
maximum; its route-cost normalization cancels when converted back to total gain. No repeated-sum
or sorting error is found. Candidate limitation, uncalibrated structural residuals and the observation–
coverage tradeoff remain distinct explanations. Calibrating structural priors against actual new support
is a proposed method change, not an implemented correction or a result of this experiment.

Problem Formulation

Each acquisition task takes place in a static environment; changes occur between tasks. The local
inspection target is one equipment-scale facility with an unknown configuration h ∈ {0, 1}. Both
policies receive two public templates, a facility coordinate frame, an initial pose s0 , and a safe pose
graph. They receive RGB-D, planar scan, and pose observations only when those observations are
acquired. The category–configuration association is manually specified public knowledge. Evaluation references, true configuration identifiers, and actual measurements from unexecuted actions are
unavailable to the planner.
A forward motion of 1 m or a 90◦ turn consumes one action and acquires a sensor frame. An
identical 18-action observation prefix precedes autonomous selection within total budget B. The original confirmation uses B = 42; the extension also evaluates 30 and 54. A qualified task must remain
collision-free, finish within budget, attain at least 80% planar coverage, and return to the initial position and orientation. The experiment consequently tests allocation after common observation, rather
than unknown-environment exploration from the first action.

Category support and geometric correction

A category cue must influence early decisions without preventing later contradictory measurements
from changing the route. We therefore register finite category support once and combine it with
bounded evidence from newly acquired viewpoints. This design allows a cue to establish a preference
while preserving both configuration hypotheses.

pixels at each of two distinct XY positions, without a competing marker in those frames. Registration
supplies Lts = ± log 9; repeated detections do not multiply this prior, and conflicting category support
clears it. G uses Lts = 0. Synthetic markers provide a controlled category channel, independently of
natural-image recognition performance.

15.4

Budgeted execution and reconstruction

An informative view is useful only if the robot can execute it and still return within budget. The
execution layer reserves the shortest graph return cost before each action; the reconstruction layer
then integrates only the depth acquired at the executed pose.
The return-feasibility check accepts a legal edge only if
1 + dreturn (s0 ) ≤ bt ,
where return distance includes restoration of the starting heading. On the fixed graph with unit
action costs, this preserves an affordable return path: after each accepted action, dreturn (st+1 ) ≤ bt −1 =
bt+1 . This certificate concerns graph feasibility; task qualification additionally requires measured
coverage, collision-free execution, and actual return.

Input: common graph, templates, budget B, prefix, policy G or S
Initialize belief support, exposures, and visited poses
Repeat:
Read the initial frame or latest executed-action frame
Fuse acquired depth exactly once into the measured TSDF
Register category support and update geometric evidence
Add current template exposures and record the measured pose
If collision or budget exhaustion: record termination; exit
If the shared prefix is unfinished: select its next action
Otherwise:
Exclude measured poses from diagnostic candidates
Solve V1 from the current state and remaining budget
If infeasible or a resource cap is exceeded: fail; exit
If stopping is optimal within tolerance: stop; exit
Select the first maximizing action in fixed graph-edge order
Check graph legality and the return-budget inequality
If rejected: record the termination reason; exit
Execute one action, charge its cost, and obtain the next frame
Output: measured map, trajectory, observations, and stop reason

Equal-valued choices use a 10−12 tolerance and fixed edge order. The shared Open3D CPU TSDF
[6, 7] uses 4 cm voxels and 12 cm truncation; public templates never contribute synthetic depth.
Outputs precede independent endpoint evaluation. Incorrect-prior controls exchange category interpretation; the no-correction variant additionally disables measured correction and forecast diagnosis.
For N poses and exposure bitset lengths m0 , m1 , the cached state count is conservatively bounded
by K ≤ 3N(B + 1)2m0 +m1 , covering the initial and two post-diagnosis weights. With maximum outdegree d, bitset-update cost Tm , and terminal-evaluation cost Tz , planning costs O(K(dTm + Tz )), excluding return-distance preprocessing. Each call permits 1.5 million cached states and approximately
20 s; elapsed time is checked every 1024 new states and at selection completion. This finite implementation supports the controlled task, with exponential exposure-state dependence.

Decision interpretation and conditions for benefit

A recorded example connects the recursion to robot behavior. After the common prefix in P00,
G assigns both configurations weight 0.5; left/right continuation values of 0.650944 tie. With
configuration-1 category support 0.9, S obtains 0.668008/0.711865 and turns right at action 19. A
misleading prior can subsequently reverse: geometric evidence of −6 at step 28 gives configuration-1
weight 1 − σ (−6 + log 9) = 0.978178, followed by a changed action at step 29. These are planning
values and belief weights; measured reconstruction outcomes are reported separately.
The underlying tradeoff can be expressed using established belief-based decision principles [12,
13]. Assume two initially equally likely configurations, two return-feasible routes, matched utility
U+ , mismatched utility U− , and ∆ = U+ −U− > 0. A category cue has symmetric reliability rc ≥ 1/2.
Optional geometric diagnosis has symmetric reliability rd ≥ 1/2, is conditionally independent given
configuration, and costs utility κ. Both routes remain feasible afterward. For current belief p, direct
choice succeeds with probability m = max(p, 1 − p); optimal choice after diagnosis succeeds with
probability max(m, rd ). Its net value is
∆ max(0, rd − m) − κ.
G benefits from diagnosis when κ < (rd − 1/2)∆, whereas a category-conditioned decision requires κ < (rd − rc )+ ∆, with κ ≥ 0. An informative cue can therefore avoid a diagnostic detour while
preserving a useful observation direction. Its benefit disappears when geometry already resolves the
choice or route utilities coincide. When rd ≥ rc and diagnosis is free, both policies can attain the same
expected directional utility; a free but less reliable diagnosis need not remove the category advantage.
These analytical reliabilities are assumptions distinct from implementation settings. The argument
identifies conditions for improved allocation; it does not guarantee better measured reconstruction
under the exposure surrogate.
The practical consequence is a conditional experimental prediction: a category cue should matter
when it changes a consequential route ranking before equivalent geometric evidence is affordable.
If geometry and category already favor the same route, identical actions and quality are expected.
Tests therefore examine action changes, acquired surfaces, and measured quality together, rather than
interpreting stronger belief alone as a mapping improvement.

Experiments and Results

The controlled experiments test category-conditioned observation at matched coverage, geometric
correction, and sensitivity to budget and changed backgrounds. Sections 17 and 18 evaluate unknownexploration and physical deployment separately.

16.1

Experimental setup and matched comparisons

The simulator supplies 96×72 depth images over a 4 m range and a 360◦ planar scan with 180 beams
over 8 m. Poses are exact. Depth noise has a standard deviation of 0.25 pixels in reference disparity; no additional random scan noise is applied. The experiments isolate planning under controlled
observations rather than reproduce physical sensor errors.
The evaluator derives the start-reachable safe-cell set R using a 0.2 m grid and 0.2 m robot radius.
It contains 588 cells in P00 and 668 in P01. A nonunknown cell counts as observed, including one
marked occupied, so coverage is not occupancy-classification accuracy. Ground truth is used after
prediction sealing, not to supply runtime measurements.
Surface precision and recall follow distance-threshold comparison [14]. Precision queries the
reference from sampled predicted surfaces; recall queries the prediction from fixed exterior vertical
reference surfaces. The prediction is cropped to the common bounding box of both templates with

31

a 0.20 m margin, and the known ground is removed. Thus, errors outside this region do not enter
precision. No registration or hole filling is applied. Thresholds are fixed at 2, 5, and 10 cm, with 5
cm primary; empty predictions receive zero quality. Coverage, precision, recall, and task qualification
accompany the product to avoid hiding its components.
Confirmation freezes the controller and uses a held-out noise seed on two previously seen parent
layouts, each with two configurations: eight runs and four G/S pairs. The layout-level units are the
two parent layouts. Two configurations per layout and repeated noise realizations are paired conditions, not independent evidence of category-level generalization. The policies differ as follows: G has
no category support; S uses the designated correct cue; X reverses the category interpretation; and
Xnf uses the same reversed interpretation while disabling measured correction and future diagnostic
anticipation. The historical development X/Xnf contrast therefore evaluates the bundled correction
policy; the separate factorial below isolates its two switches. Replays and alternative thresholds reuse
these trajectories; they do not increase the number of independent layouts or trials. The principal G
control retains the diagnostic views and revisiting available to S; Xnf deliberately disables measured
correction and anticipated diagnosis.
For each matched condition we report the absolute score difference, ∆J5 = J5,S − J5,G . A relative
mean change is 100(J 5,S /J 5,G − 1), rather than an average of per-condition percentages. Qualification
is reported over all declared attempts; endpoint means include only the explicitly qualified groups.
The recorded software is Python 3.12.3, NumPy 1.26.4, SciPy 1.11.4, and Open3D 0.19.0, with
one OpenMP, OpenBLAS, and MKL thread. No GPU is required. Figures use actual recorded actions
and measured meshes except the explicitly schematic architecture diagram.

Object-level interval correction under geometric mismatch

Scope and independent units. The conditional depth model can assign narrow intervals despite
geometric model error. We evaluated a standard split-conformal correction [18] as a reporting diagnostic, keeping the unreweighted neutral B8 predictor (structural temperature T = 1), its σ = 0.01
depth distribution, and its planning policy fixed. The new set contains 48 independently drawn procedural objects: 24 for calibration and 24 for a single held-out evaluation. Both splits follow the same
frozen mixture of closed and open structures, regular panels and declared part mismatches, with no
rejection or redraw. Each object provides front, front-and-side, around, and occluded observations,
giving 192 dependent cases and 576 frames in total. Rendering uses exact poses, noiseless first-hit
depth and ideal visible instance/category labels. The target for each case is the true object’s extent
projected onto the inferred depth axis; axis errors remain part of the evaluation. Generator truth is
unavailable to inference. A separate pair with identical acquired fronts and different hidden depths
produces exactly equal particle banks, likelihoods and posteriors; this fixture adds no independent
object to the test set.
