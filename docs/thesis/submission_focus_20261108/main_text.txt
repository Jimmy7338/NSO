Category-Conditioned Observation in Joint Unknown-Space
Exploration and Reconstruction
Anonymous research draft, 20 November 2026
Abstract
Unknown-environment exploration must discover accessible space while acquiring views that
reconstruct its surfaces. We study a joint planner that adds category-conditioned structural observation value to inherited unknown-coverage and measured-surface terms. Candidate routes and
mapping use acquired observations; hypothetical closed/open surfaces guide selection but never
enter the measured reconstruction. An exact score decomposition identifies when category probabilities can alter route rankings. A conditional depth-and-placement model and an independently
selected structural temperature address overconfident geometric evidence while preserving shared
candidates and execution rules. On 24 held-out procedural objects, neutral structural negative log
likelihood decreases from 5.0804 to 0.6791, although class accuracy is unchanged and aroundobject loss worsens. Complete exploration tests use two furniture permutations of one floorplan
with exact poses and ideal visible instance labels. All matched runs attain full correct-free coverage, return and record zero simulated collisions within 256 actions. The calibrated semantic
policy changes one complete trajectory, but reaches full coverage nine actions later, travels 1.2 m
farther and loses 0.377 percentage points of surface F1; the other permutation ties. Separate
native-candidate branches demonstrate a category-induced object-surface gain accompanied by
lower whole-scene surface gain. These findings establish controlled exploration feasibility, improved structural probability loss and conditional semantic decision effects. They do not establish
a general semantic efficiency advantage, natural-detector performance or physical reconstruction
accuracy. Full failures, adverse comparisons and independent object counts are retained.

Keywords: unknown-space exploration, active reconstruction, category priors, structural calibration,
observation planning

Introduction

Unknown-environment exploration must discover accessible space while acquiring enough views to
reconstruct its surfaces. These objectives overlap but are not interchangeable. A robot can map a
corridor while missing the back of a cabinet or the interior of a shelf; inspecting those surfaces can
also delay exploration elsewhere. The practical question is how category information should affect
observation allocation when both the map and object structure remain incomplete.
We study a joint unknown-coverage and measured-surface planner in which category votes weight
closed and open structural alternatives. Candidate routes come from acquired occupancy and surface
data, and each executed motion is followed by a new observation. The measured map remains separate
from hypothetical completion. A neutral class-prior control G and a category-conditioned policy S
share instance associations, geometry, routes, mapping and execution at any identical observed state.
This comparison isolates the class-prior contribution within the declared frontend; it does not compare
semantic perception against a wholly geometric sensing system.
A recurring failure is that available category information produces no target change. We make this
behavior explicit: the semantic score difference is the class-probability difference multiplied by each
1

route’s difference in predicted structural observation opportunity. If geometric evidence is overconfident, the two structural predictions expose similar area, or the score change does not overcome the
ranking margin, G and S choose the same route. A target change is only an intermediate result; it must
improve actual coverage or reconstruction to support a task-level advantage.
Our evaluation separates complete exploration from probability calibration and short-branch
mechanism tests. Complete simulated runs cover two furniture permutations of one branching
layout from a single initial sensor packet. Independent procedural objects test conditional extent
and structural probability, with fitting and evaluation separated by object. Finally, original candidate
branches under correct, neutral and swapped cues test how a changed prior alters actual surface
acquisition. Earlier supplied-graph studies and physical integration pilots are retained as supporting
evidence with their own assumptions.
The contributions are threefold. First, an implementation-traced joint planning formulation separates acquired mapping, hypothetical structural reward and realized execution outcomes. Second, an
exact score decomposition explains why semantic probabilities can change while the selected target
remains fixed, and motivates independently testing structural evidence scale. Third, a matched evaluation connects probability changes to native candidate choices and complete exploration, retaining
failures, ties and adverse tradeoffs. The current evidence establishes exploration feasibility and improved structural probability loss on a held-out procedural set. It does not establish a general semantic
advantage in unknown-space coverage or reconstruction.

Related Work

Active mapping selects motion to improve an environmental representation while accounting for estimation [2]. Hierarchical systems such as TARE [3] and Active Neural SLAM [4] separate strategic
exploration from local execution. Our virtual study uses the existing observed-state route generator
and primitive executor to examine the additional value of a category-conditioned surface reward. Exact poses remove localization uncertainty from that comparison; they do not validate physical SLAM
accuracy.
Reconstruction-driven next-best-view planning optimizes surface acquisition; GenNBV [5] learns
a generalizable policy in a larger viewpoint space. Semantic exploration and inspection methods use
category or scene structure to prioritize observations [8–11]. Here the comparison concerns two explicit structural alternatives on a ground robot’s available routes. Measured surfaces are integrated
volumetrically [6, 7], while predicted geometry affects only observation selection. Adapted mechanisms and historical native-system integration results in the supplement are not rankings of those
complete original systems.
Belief-based planning and robotic information gathering provide the decision background [12, 13].
The present loss-based shape weights follow a generalized updating approach [15]; the structuraltemperature adjustment uses standard probability calibration [17]. Conditional depth CRPS, class
NLL and interval coverage assess different properties. We therefore evaluate them separately and treat
a standard split-conformal interval correction [18] as a diagnostic, not a new navigation algorithm or
a guarantee of improved reconstruction.

Problem Formulation: Joint Unknown-Space Exploration and Reconstruction

We consider a ground robot that must discover a previously unmapped static environment and reconstruct its observed surfaces. The robot starts from one acquired RGB-D and planar-scan packet and
subsequently receives measurements only after executed actions. It is not supplied with the environment’s complete occupancy map, a complete safe pose graph, hidden object bounds, or source meshes.

4.1

Joint Planning with Acquired Structural Evidence
Measured state and conditional completion

The mapping backend integrates only acquired RGB-D and scan measurements. Measured occupancy
supplies traversability and unknown-space masks; an Open3D CPU TSDF supplies the reconstructed
surfaces [6, 7]. Observed instance tracks associate measured surface patches with objects. Template
completion is restricted to these positive tracks, while unassigned geometry still contributes to mapping, collision checking and the common coverage/quality terms. This prevents a small unassigned
fragment from independently receiving a whole furniture template. Both G and S receive the same
ideal visible instance associations in the virtual tests.
Each modeled object has closed-box and open-shelf alternatives. Their common coordinate frame,
width, vertical parts and measured support are estimated from acquired points. Qualified outer faces
constrain orientation; measured horizontal faces constrain available layers. These estimates remain
imperfect. We represent uncertainty only in local depth and the allocation of unseen extent; orientation, width, layer count and pose are not integrated out. Model mismatch is therefore retained rather
than absorbed into a claim of a complete shape posterior.
For object o, geometric particle k = (d, a) has depth d and an allocation a of unseen extent around
the measured interval. The public depth density is uniform on [0.2, 1.4] m, conditioned on the acquired span. Nodes at 0.1 m spacing, together with an inserted measured-span boundary, receive
clipped Voronoi integration masses. A qualified measured end fixes that coordinate. Without an end
anchor, a ∈ {0, 0.5, 1} has prior masses {0.25, 0.5, 0.25} and places the lower endpoint at measured
minimum minus a(d − span). Opposite observed ends fix depth directly. Out-of-support spans and
missing/multiple tracks remain explicit failures in independent probability evaluation; its public-prior
fallback is an evaluator convention, not hidden ground-truth object selection.

4.4

Decision–execution–observation feedback

The original heading-state local planner produces a sequence of forward and turn primitives for the
selected observation option. Before each primitive, the common execution layer checks current acquired traversability and the remaining action/return contract. The next observation updates measured
mapping and realized gain estimates; an invalidated option is replanned from that state. Global reselection occurs at option completion or invalidation, not after every frame. The declared virtual runs
retain the original collision and return checks without target blacklists, additional candidate filters or
a learned reachability network.
Unknown-exploration loop. Acquire the initial packet; update the measured map and instance
support. Generate feasible observation routes from acquired state. Construct a common geometric
bank and score each route with coverage, measured quality and expected completion. Select the
highest positive route under the shared tie rule. Execute a checked primitive, charge its cost and
acquire a new packet. Continue the option while valid; otherwise replan. Return according to the
declared budget contract, and retain all failures and actual observations for independent evaluation.

5.1

Experiments and Results
Evidence levels and matched exploration protocol

The primary complete-exploration comparison uses two inspected furniture permutations of one synthetic branching floorplan, with textured assets and partial ideal visible instance/category labels. Each
run begins with one actual simulated packet, giving 42.606% correct-free coverage; the remaining
map is unknown. Exact poses, a 0.2 m circular radius, 120×90 RGB-D at 90◦ field of view, 8 m sensing range and 180 planar scan beams are shared. The measured TSDF uses 6 cm voxels and 12 cm
truncation. The 256-action cap includes return and is not shortened at first complete coverage, because
surface acquisition can continue after floor exploration.
Four new T = 16 G/S runs are compared with four frozen T = 1 distribution runs on these same
layouts. Temperature was selected only on independent object classification loss. The complete-policy
trigger was any changed target in the six fixed prefixes, irrespective of whether its actual branch outcome improved. It was met at one prefix; no new candidate, score coefficient, budget or stopping rule
was introduced. Independent evaluation checks the complete action and sensor records, source hashes
and native measured meshes. There are no random repeats or independent floorplan replications in
this comparison.
Other experiments answer narrower questions. An earlier 24-object split (12 training, six validation, six held-out) selects the conditional-depth scale. A new 48-object set (24 calibration, 24 test)
independently evaluates structural temperature and a report-only interval correction. Four viewing
conditions per object are dependent observations of that object. Six retained exploration prefixes and
66 previously acquired outbound branches support repeated model diagnostics; reusing them does not
increase sample count. The final cue mechanism uses eight separate native outbound branches reused
across twelve score conditions. Supplied-graph observation and real-robot pilots have different state
and execution assumptions and are retained separately in the supplement.

5.3

Independent structural calibration and its limits

The structural study fixes 24 new calibration and 24 new test objects before observing predictions,
using the same IID mixture of closed/open objects and predeclared geometry mismatch. Each object
is measured from front, front–side, around-object and occluded conditions. Generated first-hit depth
and visible labels are passed to the actor; hidden dimensions and source meshes are reserved for
generation and evaluation. The inferred-axis projection of true geometry defines the depth target, so
axis errors are not mislabeled as successful depth estimation. All eight multiple-track fallbacks remain
in the test denominator.
Neutral class NLL selects T = 16 on the calibration objects. On the held-out test objects, neutral
NLL falls from 5.0804 to 0.6791, Brier score from 0.3251 to 0.2363, and wrong predictions above
0.9 confidence from 27 to 3. Neutral accuracy remains 63.02%, consistent with preserving its class
ordering. With a supplied correct 9:1 cue, S∗ reaches NLL 0.1393 and 96.875% class accuracy; this is
a controlled-prior condition, not an automatic-recognition result. Around-object neutral NLL worsens
from 0.0829 to 0.3414, and neutral depth median error increases. The complete condition table and
calibration/test rows are retained in the supplement.
These results identify excessive structural confidence as one cause of suppressed category influence, but not the only cause of G/S ties. In the preceding distribution runs, G/S scores differ at seven
of 30 left and six of 26 right planning calls while their selected candidates remain identical. Eq. 6
explains why probability and score changes need not cross the route-ranking margin. The temperature revision makes a category-dependent choice executable, but Table 1 shows no coverage-efficiency
benefit.
Conditional extent probability also has important limits. The earlier depth scale was chosen from
eight values by training CRPS; 0.01 m is the grid’s lower endpoint, not a demonstrated globally optimal scale. Informative-view CRPS improves in its independent validation/held-out objects, while
nominal 95% intervals under-cover. A separate standard object-level split-conformal diagnostic on
the new 24/24 objects raises test case coverage from 61/96 to 93/96 and simultaneous four-condition
object coverage from 5/24 to 21/24. Mean interval width increases from 0.278 to 2.316 m and interval
score worsens from 2.140 to 2.704. Its observed object coverage is 87.5%, not an empirical 95%
success claim. This correction applies only to the unreweighted T = 1 neutral distribution, leaves all
probability masses unchanged and is not used for planning.

5.4

Controlled category effects on native observation branches

In two procedural object arrangements, correct, neutral and swapped category cues are evaluated at
T = 1 and T = 16 using the original candidate generator. Within each arrangement all cues share
exactly the same acquired input, particle bank, common score and four candidates. The four available
candidates all lie to the right; the experiment therefore tests their actual ranking, not a designed symmetric left/right choice. Across arrangements, rendered depth differs by at most 5 × 10−7 m, so they
are not exact sensor counterfactuals. Eight native outbound routes are executed once and their outcomes reused for the twelve score conditions; all eight reach their endpoints over 114 actions without
simulated collision.
At T = 16 in the right arrangement, the correct cue changes the original target and increases object
net-new surface by 1.3565 m2 , improves correctly free coverage gain by 3.328 percentage points and
uses two fewer actions. Whole-scene net-new surface simultaneously decreases by 24.1218 m2 . The
left arrangement ties at T = 16; at T = 1, its correct cue reduces object surface gain while increasing
whole-scene gain. These complete contrasts demonstrate that category input can influence native
observation allocation, with substantial objective tradeoffs. They do not demonstrate a completeexploration or uniformly positive semantic effect.

5.5

Supporting controlled and physical evidence

A reduced supplied-graph study uses public facility templates, a shared 18-action prefix and a 42action reference budget. It reports two improvements and two ties in four reference pairs, and a 6.16%
mean joint coverage–surface gain at unchanged planar coverage. Changed-background tests include
one improvement and three ties; an independent correction factorial finds a 2.66% gain from measured
correction and no trajectory effect from forecast diagnosis. Planning failures and a larger-budget
reversal remain included. These are conditional observation-allocation mechanisms, not unknownspace initialization or stopping rules. Their full formulation, recursion and results are archived in the
supplement.
The real-robot record includes completed short joint-exploration episodes, failed returns, control
interruptions and map drift. A fresh-map ordered C/S pilot has different initial observations and a
failed S return, preventing a matched efficiency ranking. Internal scan unions and quality proxies
lack independent geometric ground truth. The physical implementation therefore provides integration
evidence; it does not validate the simulated semantic comparison, SLAM accuracy or the complete
temperature policy. Hardware experiments are paused following platform damage.

6

Discussion and Limitations

The main unresolved issue is the gap between predicted observation value and the joint task outcome.
Softer structural evidence restores the influence of a finite category prior, and native branches show that
this influence can change actual surface acquisition. Yet an object-focused gain can displace a larger
whole-scene gain or delay unknown coverage. The complete left-layout comparison demonstrates that
this tradeoff can persist beyond the first changed target. A better probability loss is therefore useful
model evidence, not a substitute for coverage and reconstruction evaluation.
Geometry remains conditional and imperfect. The particle model integrates depth and placement
while retaining estimated orientation, width and parts. Ideal instance labels do not remove these geometric errors. The earlier fixed-state audits identify unassigned fragments receiving whole templates,
unreliable outer-plane fits, unobserved depth and imperfect layer placement; their failures remain in
the supplement. Independent procedural measurements have exact poses and noiseless depth, and the
two exploration layouts were inspected during development. Neither independent object tests nor multiple policy runs convert these related floorplan permutations into broad environment generalization.
The class-temperature adjustment and split-conformal interval correction are standard calibration
tools applied to distinct targets. Positive temperature cannot correct the sign of neutral structural
evidence, while wide residual intervals can improve coverage without improving usefulness. The
interval diagnostic’s mean width exceeds 2 m and its proper interval score worsens. Nominal interval
coverage, class calibration and navigation utility must consequently remain separate claims. The
matched G control also shares ideal instance association; it should not be interpreted as a semanticsfree perception baseline.
Computational and physical limits remain substantial. Policies use a logical action budget and
exact virtual poses; three concurrent runs with numerical-library thread limits of one do not establish
real-time robot control. Native measured reconstruction is evaluated against a fixed source reference,
without predicted geometry insertion or post-hoc alignment. The physical records lack such a reference and include localization/control failures. Deployment with estimated poses, natural detections
and robust execution requires a separate matched evaluation after hardware recovery.

7

Conclusion

We have formulated and tested joint unknown-space coverage and surface observation with a categoryconditioned structural reward. The implementation preserves the separation between acquired maps,

9

hypothetical geometry and execution outcomes. Its score decomposition makes semantic inactivity
diagnosable: changing a class probability matters only when structural observation opportunities and
route margins permit a different target.
Independent structural calibration reduces probability loss, and complete simulated runs establish full-coverage feasibility under the declared action and return contract. Category information can
change native target selection, but the complete comparison remains adverse in one permutation and
tied in the other. The evidence therefore supports an active-planning system and a concrete semantic
decision mechanism, while leaving a general coverage or reconstruction advantage unproven. Future
improvements should be judged by complete unknown-exploration outcomes alongside object and
whole-scene surface acquisition, rather than by stronger probabilities or selected favorable branches.

Reproducibility and Data

The primary policy traces to the frozen v10_3_1 observed-state scoring/runtime source and its declared joint-exploration adapter. The common coverage and measured-quality terms, original candidates, heading-state local planner and execution contract are retained in the compared arms. Separate
frozen modules specify conditional geometry and structural-temperature changes. Historical interface names OV-SDF, STGHP, RPN-UQ and IGCR do not imply that a trained reachability network or
complete original supplied-graph controller was active in these virtual runs.
The accompanying sources contain object-level splits, generated sensor manifests, sanitized actor inputs, frozen method/configuration hashes, all candidate scores, acquired action records, native
meshes and independent evaluation receipts. Public summary tables identify reused controls and
branches. No test-set score, hidden source mesh or future observation is supplied to the actor. Python
file-access audit hooks provide provenance checks for trusted code; they are not an operating-system
security sandbox. The supplement preserves earlier controlled results, null/negative exploration comparisons, rejected geometry variants and physical failures. Author identities and submission details
remain unfilled.
