\section{Matched policies with conditional depth}
\label{sec:joint-depth-policy}

\paragraph{Controls and fixed-prefix prediction.}
The five declared arms are G fixed, S fixed, G distribution, S distribution
and S mean collapse. Every arm uses the same conditional non-depth geometry,
observed-instance support, joint loss update and frozen scale
$\sigma=0.01$\,m. Fixed mode retains the location-allocation hypotheses and
their update; its unresolved depth is $\max(0.65\,\mathrm{m},s)$, while
credible opposing faces retain their measured extent. It is therefore a
matched extent control, not the preceding O policy or the original fixed
template implementation. Mean collapse first computes the full S joint
distribution, retains each structure's probability, and replaces that
structure's geometry distribution by its conditional mean depth and lower
coordinate. It does not recompute the likelihood or class weights.

The same six acquired development prefixes and all 66 original measured
candidate branches provide 330 forecasts, without new branch executions.
The input geometry and actual branch outcomes are shared across arms.
Table~\ref{tab:joint-depth-replay} uses equal-state averages after averaging
candidates within a state. Relative to S fixed, S distribution increases
absolute source-supported forecast weight from 0.3600 to 0.4368\,m$^2$,
realized forecast weight from 0.1623 to 0.2216\,m$^2$, and rank correlation
from 0.787 to 0.835. These improvements are not uniform across states, and
mean collapse has still greater realized weight (0.2448\,m$^2$) and rank
(0.849). Within each of the fixed and distribution modes, G and S select
the same candidate at all six states. Only the left state after 54 actions
changes its choice between fixed and distribution modes: it trades slightly
less actual object/global net surface for fewer actions and a shorter path.
The six prefixes have already been inspected during method development;
neither 330 forecasts nor 66 reused branches represent independent scenes.

\paragraph{Complete unknown exploration.}
All five arms run in both existing furniture permutations with the unchanged
unknown-coverage and measured-quality scores, candidates, sensor fusion,
256-action budget and return rules. They share the same acquired prefix,
whose correct-free coverage is 42.606\%. The ten complete runs all reach
100\% correct-free coverage, return and incur zero simulated collisions
(Table~\ref{tab:joint-depth-policy}). Their native measured TSDFs are
evaluated independently; predicted templates are never fused as observations.

On the left layout, distribution mode reaches full coverage at action 88
rather than 97 and travels 42.0 rather than 43.2\,m. Final 5\,cm F1 increases
from 92.381\% to 92.758\%. Cabinet recall increases by 2.834 percentage
points, while shelf recall decreases by 1.294 points. This effect occurs
equally for G and S. Mean collapse reproduces the complete distribution
trajectory and TSDF exactly. On the right, all five trajectories and TSDFs
are identical. Thus the matched comparison demonstrates a benefit from
replacing the fixed extent in one inspected layout; it does not isolate a
benefit of keeping geometric spread or using the semantic class prior.
Historical O outputs are references, not additional new trials or matched
controls for the changed joint update.

The category pathway is active despite the tied policies. Paired G/S scores
differ by more than $10^{-12}$ at 4/27 left and 5/26 right planning states in
fixed mode, and at 7/30 left and 6/26 right states in distribution mode.
None of these score differences changes the selected candidate.

Distribution mode also costs more computation: its elapsed times are
417.8--500.4\,s, compared with 274.2--292.8\,s for fixed mode and
259.5--297.3\,s for mean collapse. These are wall times under three concurrent
processes, each restricted to one numerical-library thread, on a host with
eight available CPU cores. The budget counts logical simulated actions;
these times do not measure real-robot motion or real-time control throughput.
The study has one floorplan family, two related furniture permutations and
no stochastic repeats. The controlled coverage result, conditional surface
tradeoff, semantic tie and extra distribution cost are all retained.
