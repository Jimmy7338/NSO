\subsection{Calibrating area with independent fitted furniture}
We constructed eight single-object room layouts with distinct furniture assemblies. Each starts from a frontal RGB-D prefix and supplies three executed branches of 13 actions. Four layouts calibrate the estimator and four held-out layouts evaluate it. The B11 layouts do not enter either split. The original visibility and acquisition forecasts share the fitted geometry posterior. We split the acquisition forecast's gross-new area at a distance of 0.25\,m from the corresponding acquired instance support. On the calibration layouts, we minimize layout-weighted MAE over two multipliers in $[0,1]$ with the farther multiplier bounded by the nearer one. The fitted multipliers are 0.45 and 0.07. A single-multiplier reference uses 0.12. These coefficients are fixed before executing the held-out layouts.

All 24 branches reach their endpoints without collisions. On the 12 held-out branches, the two-multiplier correction reduces the acquisition MAE from 0.5722 to 0.1994\,m$^2$. Signed bias decreases from $+0.5541$ to $+0.0014$\,m$^2$. MAE falls relative to uncorrected acquisition in all four layouts and relative to single-multiplier scaling in two. The number of correctly ordered pairs rises from six to seven out of twelve. The top-ranked branch stays the same in all four layouts, with mean realized gross and net gains of 0.3592 and 0.3418\,m$^2$. In one held-out cabinet layout, the corrected forecast for its highest-ranked branch remains 0.6273\,m$^2$ against 0.0897\,m$^2$ measured. The best measured branch produces 0.6302\,m$^2$. This fixed-route experiment assesses area prediction and ranking of the template term on ideal simulated inputs. Its results leave the effect on the combined planner undetermined.
