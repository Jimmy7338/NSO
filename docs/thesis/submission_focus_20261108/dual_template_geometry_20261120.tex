\section{Template support and complete candidate acquisition}
\label{sec:staged-template-geometry}

\paragraph{Separate model revisions.}
We evaluate three cumulative changes to B2 while retaining its visibility correction, common coverage and measured-quality scores, candidates, coefficient and measured-only TSDF. First, all template quadrature points are retained and history changes their weights through a mask. This removes the global 3,500-point thinning operation. Second, acquired face normals estimate an orientation modulo $90^\circ$, and opposing measured planes constrain horizontal extents. A single observed face anchors the inherited extent prior; its unobserved endpoint remains unresolved. Measured upper-plane support can move the height estimate outside the inherited 0.9--1.5~m bounds; unobserved geometry remains unresolved. This finite envelope estimator retains the grounded-object assumption and fixed box/shelf parts; it is not full shape optimization or a calibrated distribution over unseen geometry. G and S share the estimated geometry.

Third, we add free-space evidence to the structure pseudo-likelihood. For each hypothesis $h$, let $A_h^f$ and $A_h^m$ denote template quadrature area supported as strictly free or measured surface by the acquired keyframes. Set $f_h=A_h^f/(A_h^f+A_h^m)$, or zero when neither type of support exists. The shelf log odds receive the additional term
\[
\Delta\ell_f=(f_{\rm box}-f_{\rm shelf})\frac{0.15^2}{2(0.06)^2}.
\]
The scales come from the inherited distance pseudo-likelihood. Finite positive depth is required; the closed $\pm0.10$~m surface band vetoes conflicting free evidence. Invalid, occluded and unobserved samples add no evidence, and repeated frames do not multiply a sample's contribution. Category priors remain the only G/S difference. The resulting weights are heuristic pseudo-posteriors.

After this diagnostic, a second declared revision addresses duplicated templates and unsupported probability mass using the same acquired ideal-label frontend. Acquired fragments assigned to the same tracked instance are pooled before constructing one box/shelf pair; unknown-instance groups remain separate. Let $q$ denote the shelf probability after the category prior and measured-surface update, and $p$ its value after the relative free-space update. With $K=3.125$, set
\[
Z=(1-q)e^{-Kf_{\rm box}}+qe^{-Kf_{\rm shelf}},\qquad
(m_{\rm box},m_{\rm shelf})=Z(1-p,p).
\]
The residual $1-Z$ is heuristic accounting for unmodeled mass, not a fitted third structure class; it creates no surface. The inherited conditional log-odds clipping remains in $p$. This heuristic discounts both hypotheses when both conflict with observed free space, without asserting that unknown geometry validates either template. Geometry estimation and absolute attenuation are crossed in four pooled-instance variants. No tracked group is excluded by inherited size eligibility at the six states.

\paragraph{Fixed inputs and actual candidate outcomes.}
Before execution, the protocol fixes states 24/54/109 from the original left-layout S route and 24/53/110 from the right route, including every recorded candidate. Prefix replay matches original packets, poses, instance association, quality counts, feedback and scores. Each of 66 branches starts from a byte-identical reconstruction of its acquired prefix and executes its fixed outbound actions. All endpoints are reached in 1,569 total branch actions with zero simulated collisions. All eight model versions share those acquired outcomes; no model chooses new actions during a branch. The second diagnostic reconstructs and verifies each prefix, then reuses the original branch meshes and receipts without acquiring new branches. These are dependent development diagnostics from two permutations of one floorplan, not 66 autonomous episodes or independent environments. Reference geometry is used only by the subsequent evaluator.

\paragraph{Prediction consistency and decision consequences.}
Full-basis integration reproduces B2 gains and choices at all six normal-history states, which did not activate the old cap. For S, geometric estimation lowers the mean source-supported fraction of predicted reward from 36.45\% to 29.16\%, while adding free-space evidence recovers it to 30.13\%. Absolute source-supported and realized weights also remain below B2 (Table~\ref{tab:staged-template-geometry}); this is not an improvement obtained by discarding predicted mass. Mean within-state rank association rises from 0.702 to 0.722, but the two geometry variants change the full-score choice only at left state 109. There, G selects an 18-action branch adding an estimated 0.633~m$^2$ object surface and 3.388~m$^2$ global surface; S selects a 47-action branch adding 0.486 and 4.739~m$^2$, respectively. These are net changes after subtracting lost surface matches. Both add zero coverage at this already covered state. The other five selected candidates remain unchanged for F, F+M and F+M+E in both G and S.

Across the six states, selected global net area averages 16.161~m$^2$ for B2 G/S, 15.968 for revised G and 16.193 for revised S; selected coverage gain remains 1.318 percentage points. The changed choice does not establish a semantic efficiency benefit.

Instance pooling alone preserves all selected candidates while reducing S rank association to 0.679. Pooling plus absolute attenuation reduces it further to 0.645. This variant slightly increases selected object net area from 1.017 to 1.050~m$^2$ and reduces mean action count from 45.2 to 39.5, but reduces selected global net area from 16.161 to 15.792~m$^2$. G and S select identical candidates in all six states. Combining pooling, geometry and attenuation lowers S rank association to 0.610 and restores every B2 choice. Thus, reduced prediction magnitude alone does not identify a better surface predictor or a semantic advantage. None of these geometric or pooled-instance variants is promoted to the conditional complete-policy comparison.

Decomposing the selected S rewards explains why free-space attenuation can suppress useful object evidence. In P+A, the labeled furniture hypotheses retain only $Z=0.070$--$0.142$, whereas some unmarked groups retain $Z=1$. The mean unmarked share of the selected predicted reward rises from 66.44\% in B2 to 73.24\% in P+A and 78.44\% in P+M+A. These are six-state means of prediction-weight fractions, not recovered physical area. A mismatched shape can conflict with observed free space even when its object is present; an unmarked hypothesis without contradictory observations can retain weight without being geometrically supported. Thus, treating unrefuted geometry as applicable does not resolve the fixed-template mismatch.

\subsection{Measured-surface consumption on a common quadrature basis}
A separately fixed six-run comparison isolates measured-surface reward consumption in the full-basis version F. G, S and S$^{-m}$ each execute a complete 256-action policy in both existing layouts. S$^{-m}$ removes only measured-surface consumption; strict valid-free-space rejection, B2 visibility, the full quadrature basis and all other policy settings remain. All six runs reach 100\% correct-free coverage, return to the anchor and record zero simulated collisions. G and S have identical trajectories and native mesh hashes in each layout, also matching their corresponding B2 records. These reruns validate implementation parity and the isolated switch; they add no independent scene or stochastic repetition.

Retaining measured-surface consumption raises terminal 5~cm F1 by 0.350 percentage points on the left and 0.060 on the right, and closed-cabinet recall by 9.486 and 0.828 points. The coverage tradeoff remains explicit: in the left layout, S reaches full coverage at action 97 compared with 67 for S$^{-m}$, although its full-budget path is 1.2~m shorter. Both right-layout arms reach full coverage at action 100 and travel 43.2~m. Open-shelf recall is slightly lower with consumption on the left and slightly higher on the right (Table~\ref{tab:clean-consumption-policy}). Thus, consumption affects observation allocation and final surface recovery without establishing faster unknown-space coverage or a semantic advantage.

The left S$^{-m}$ run has two scoring calls whose retained points exceed the former 3,500-point cap; all are integrated. The earlier no-history rows removed both measured and free-space history and used state-dependent thinning, so they remain separate descriptive results. The new contrast isolates measured-surface consumption with identical quadrature rules. Historical C/J controls are reused as declared, and retain their previously reported outcomes.
