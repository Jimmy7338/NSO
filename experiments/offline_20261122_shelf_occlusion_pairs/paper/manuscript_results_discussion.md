# Discussion

The paired design isolates the introduction of frontal occlusion in these simulated rooms. It measures recoverable surface relative to the actually fused cold-start map of each placement. Different initial visibility changes the surface available to recover, while the paired shelf samples and footprint feasibility remain fixed. The comparison tests route selection with visible class labels, exact pose and a shared neutral continuation. The two ablations localize potential effects of class conditioning and of the supported increment on measured inspection.

The retention outcome concerns the early surface samples that were both absent at the fork and exclusive to one trajectory at action 96. Continued neutral exploration may let the other trajectory observe the same surface, while fusion may cause formerly matched samples to move outside the tolerance. Either event can reduce measured exclusivity at return. Whole-scene J includes free-area coverage and surfaces outside the shelf, allowing it to respond differently to a change in shelf inspection.

The independent sample comprises six authored room–shelf units with shared shelf assets across some units. The analysis retains 4 assignment rows with a guard, early-termination or technical disposition under the registered policy. Measured denominators accompany each contrast, and unexecuted action clocks remain missing. The numerical tie tolerance was 1e-09 m², fixed from repeated development measurements. The results describe this matched simulation design with ideal visible labels. The measured intervention is an early route choice at the cold start.
